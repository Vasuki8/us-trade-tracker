# Pilot task study: owner-run guide and blank scorecard

This is a preparation kit, not a report of research findings. No participant sessions or demand validation are claimed. The owner recruits volunteers and conducts sessions; this document authorizes no outreach, recording, analytics or participant-data collection by an agent.

Use the [current pilot](https://us-trade-tracker.pages.dev/) for an exploratory session of about 20–30 minutes. Its product coverage is two coffee categories, with accepted reporting through August 2026. The Canada page is a fixed September 2024–August 2026 historical snapshot. Check the displayed coverage at the session date; do not imply that a saved view receives automatic monthly updates.

## Before the tasks: fit and recent work (about five minutes)

Ask the participant:

- What is your role, and what trade-research question did you last need to answer?
- What product, trade direction and period mattered? What did you use instead, and how did that work out?
- How often does that question arise, and what happens if the answer is wrong?
- Is researching one of the pilot's coffee categories relevant to that work, or would this only be a demonstration?

If the actual product is absent, record a coverage limitation. Do not treat an unrelated coffee demonstration as success or failure for that participant's real task. They may decline a task; use **not relevant** when appropriate. Do not ask whether they would pay as a substitute for evidence of recurring use.

## Participant prompts (about fifteen minutes)

Give one prompt at a time. Read only the applicable prompt; keep the facilitator notes, checked example and answer key out of the participant's initial view. Allow the participant to choose an import or export question relevant to their role.

**T1 — Find the right product and comparison.** “Choose the roasted or unroasted coffee category that fits your question. How would you check that it is the right classification? Find a U.S. sourcing or export comparison you could use. Explain what it measures and which periods it covers.”

**T2 — Investigate changing partner shares.** “For that product and direction, which partner countries gained or lost share? Compare what you learn from the latest month with what you learn from a twelve-month view. Explain what each share is a share of. If a comparison cannot be made, explain what is missing.”

**T3 — Investigate estimated duties, if relevant.** “For an import question, investigate how estimated duties changed. Explain the dollar amounts, the value used for the percentage, the comparison periods and any reporting limitations. What conclusions would you be comfortable sharing?” For an export-only participant, record **not relevant** and move on.

**T4 — Hand a finding to another person.** “Choose a finding you would want someone else to check. Save, share or download it in a useful form. Explain what the other person could reproduce and what context they would still need.” Optional country-research branch: “If researching U.S. trade with Canada fits your work, find a useful country comparison and explain its coverage and how current it is.” This is part of T4, not a fifth required task.

After each task ask: “What was unclear?” and “How does this compare with how you actually do this work?” Do not suggest a missing feature before hearing the answer.

## Facilitator notes — do not read as instructions to participants

Stay neutral. Observe before helping; do not name a control, point at an answer or praise a particular conclusion. If the participant is stuck, ask what they expected to happen. Give assistance only when necessary to continue, and record exactly what you supplied. A correct answer reached with help is **completed with help**. A participant correctly recognizing unavailable data can complete the task; missing data is not zero.

T1: Confirm that the participant checks the official description rather than assuming every coffee form is interchangeable. Record the chosen direction, reporting month, units and source. U.S. exports include re-exports; destination growth does not measure that destination's total worldwide demand. A dollar comparison is not a price, quantity or profitability comparison.

T2: Observe whether the participant distinguishes latest month versus the same month last year from the latest twelve months versus the preceding twelve months. Shares use the U.S. world total for the same product, direction, measure and period. A country's share change is in percentage points. The ranking and selected-country chart controls serve different views; do not assume one changes the other. Record any confusion without first teaching these distinctions. Country comparisons require complete compatible periods; unavailable countries are excluded, not assigned zero.

T3: Check the participant's interpretation after they answer. The percentage uses calculated duties divided by imports-for-consumption value, not general import value. A rolling share divides matching sums, not monthly percentage averages. Zero or missing consumption value makes the share unavailable. RP70/79 can report dutiable value without calculating duty, so reported zeros do not establish duty-free trade. Retain qualifications and affected dutiable value. Calculated duties can differ from amounts paid, exclude antidumping and countervailing duties, and do not establish an individual shipment's liability. A change does not by itself prove a tariff change; composition and reporting coverage can matter.

T4: Observe whether the saved or downloaded result includes enough product, direction, period, measure and source context. Saved coffee links select a view of the latest reviewed dataset; they do not pin immutable historical observations. Without JavaScript, static default views remain readable and query selections are not applied; note the displayed limitation. Downloaded CSV/PNG files preserve their generated content and provenance, while links can resolve to later accepted data. The Canada snapshot remains fixed historical coverage and promises no automatic refresh. A Canada link has its own direction/category selections; do not assume it shares a coffee profile's controls.

The existing routes and private facilitator answer key have been checked against the retained verified package. This author verification is not a participant session. If the live site later differs, record its displayed release identity before judging an answer. Do not use this guide to declare an unexplained numerical mismatch acceptable.

## Turn observations into a decision

Use the following rubric after a session. Several explanations can fit one observation; preserve uncertainty rather than assigning a feature immediately.

| Evidence class | What to capture | Next decision |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Reproducible correctness or accessibility failure | Exact route/view, displayed release, expected versus actual result, reproduction steps and impact | Independently reproduce; prioritize numerical/data-quality or access failures before convenience work; scope a fix separately |
| Navigation or interpretation difficulty | Where progress stopped, participant explanation, assistance and consequence | Look for recurring confusion in relevant tasks; compare a small explanation/navigation change with existing alternatives |
| Coverage limitation | Missing product, period, geography or comparable observation | Separate missing data from usability; assess evidence and cost before any separately authorized intake |
| Untested feature request | Requested outcome, current workaround and recurrence | Treat it as a hypothesis; seek a concrete task and evidence before choosing implementation |

Create a ranked evidence list with anonymous session/task references, recurrence, consequence of error, confidence, alternative explanations and a proposed next decision. Do not count repeated problems from one person as multiple independent users. One reproducible serious error can warrant action even without recurrence; frequency alone does not determine priority. The list is not an automatically approved backlog.

Start with a small first set of relevant volunteers to test the guide and expose obvious failures. This remains exploratory; the launch brief's broader objective of interviews with ten relevant businesses plus a few analysts or journalists remains open. Actual task usefulness, recurring need and commercial viability require real observations. The separate requirement to update successfully across two successive official releases also remains open.

After the tasks, the owner may discuss the [checked coffee example](coffee-sample-briefing.md). Do not show it first or score a rehearsed answer as an unaided discovery. Return anonymized observed failures, real-task context and existing alternatives when available; preparing this kit does not authorize an agent to recruit or contact anyone.
